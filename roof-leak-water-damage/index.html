Roof Leak Water Damage Help in Murray

Where you see the stain and where the rain gets in are usually two different places. A loose shingle, a failed flashing joint or an aging seal around a skylight lets rain in, and it travels across the roof deck and framing until it falls into the insulation. By the time a brown ring shows on the ceiling, the attic above it has often been wet for a while. Our team maps everything that got wet, then dries it.

To be clear, roof repair is not what we do, water damage is. We work on the interior, which means removing wet insulation, drying drywall and framing, dealing with mold and rebuilding the ceiling. When the roof is still letting water through, a temporary tarp is something we can install, and we will coordinate with your roofer so the repair comes before the ceiling is closed.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut Off Wet CircuitsIf water is dripping through a light fixture, a ceiling fan or near an outlet, switch off that circuit at the breaker and leave the fixture alone.
  2. Catch the DripsFurniture, electronics and rugs should be moved aside, with buckets placed wherever water is falling. Plastic or towels spread on the floor will keep it from soaking.
  3. Drain a Bulging CeilingIf the ceiling is holding a pocket of water, stand to one side and poke a small hole at the lowest point so it drains into a bucket.
  4. Leave the Roof AloneA wet roof and an unlit attic are both places to stay out of. Just observe the entry point from the room below, then hand the job to a trained crew.
  5. Photograph the DamageUse your phone to record the damaged ceiling, attic and belongings first, hold off on throwing things out until that is done, and call us to get the drying started.
Coverage

What About Insurance?

Homeowners policies often cover interior water damage when a roof leak is sudden and accidental, such as wind tearing off shingles or a limb puncturing the roof. Insurers generally turn down claims tied to wear, age or lack of upkeep, and they may handle the roof repair on different terms than the inside damage. We document the damage with photos and moisture readings for your adjuster, but coverage depends on your policy, so read it or ask your agent.

The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • Storm impact, such as hail or a tree branch, breaking through the shingles.
  • Skylight seals and curbs that dry out, crack and let rain run inside.
  • A valley liner that has worn through at the point where the most runoff passes.
  • A vent pipe boot whose rubber has split from long sun exposure.
  • Flashing that has rusted, lifted or pulled away around chimneys, vents and walls.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Inside the Walls

From the attic, a leak can drop into a stud bay and soak the wall insulation and drywall from behind. Often the one clue is paint that blisters or trim that swells at the floor.

Drywall Ceilings

Drywall that stays wet stains, sags and loses its strength. Joints open, tape peels, and a waterlogged piece may fall without much notice.

Rafters and Decking

Wood in the roof structure that is soaked over and over darkens, swells and softens. Decay here means less strength in what carries the roof.

Insulation in the Attic

Once soaked, insulation flattens, loses its value and keeps the drywall under it damp for days. Fiberglass can sometimes be dried. Soaked cellulose usually has to be removed.

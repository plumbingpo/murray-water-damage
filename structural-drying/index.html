No part of restoring a water-damaged building matters more than structural drying, yet it is also where untrained crews make the most mistakes. Extraction takes care of standing water, but drywall, framing, subflooring, insulation, concrete and other building materials stay loaded with trapped moisture. Controlled evaporation and dehumidification, applied scientifically, are needed to get that moisture out before a wet structure develops mold, rotting wood, degraded materials and odors.

Psychrometry is the science of how air temperature, humidity and moisture interact, and the IICRC-certified technicians who dry structures for Murray Restoration know it well. For each job they apply it to calculate airflow patterns, temperature settings and the placement of equipment that will give the best conditions for evaporation. Plaster, concrete, hardwood and drywall each dry at their own rate, so each needs its own approach to get down to an acceptable moisture level with no added damage.

Our structural drying process includes daily moisture monitoring with calibrated moisture meters, thermo-hygrometers and thermal imaging cameras. Readings are recorded from start to finish in a detailed drying log, which shows moisture dropping step by step in every affected material. Each day we use those numbers to reposition or retune the equipment so drying stays efficient. A surface that feels dry to your hand proves nothing, so the job is finished only once all affected materials test within the acceptable standards set by IICRC S500.

The Benefits of Professional Structural Drying

Drying a structure properly takes science, and guesswork has no place in it. The leading cause of secondary mold after a water event is setting out a few fans and trusting they will be enough. It involves daily monitoring, a calculation of the required grain depression (the capacity to remove moisture) and a working knowledge of the way materials absorb moisture and let it go. Lacking that understanding, people either stop too soon and leave moisture for mold, or dry too hard and harm wood and other materials.

Professional drying equipment is not a bigger version of what stores sell, it is a different kind of machine. High-velocity air movers produce precise airflow patterns that speed up evaporation from the structure, and low-grain refrigerant (LGR) commercial dehumidifiers capture 10 to 20 times the moisture a home unit can. This equipment represents a significant investment and requires training to deploy effectively, both reasons why professional drying produces far better outcomes than DIY attempts.

How to Prevent Structural Drying Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Shut the doors and windows of any room under drying, which keeps dehumidification working well
  • Avoid moving the machines yourself, because their placement follows our moisture map
  • Go by the meter, not by touch, since a dry-feeling surface may still hold moisture deep inside
  • Make sure the technician can get in each day for monitoring visits
  • Keep belongings off the machines and out of the path of the air movers
  • Address any water intrusion immediately, because the faster drying begins, the better the outcome

Signs It Is Time for Structural Drying

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Beads of moisture on windows and cold surfaces after the water event
  • Materials breaking down in a spot that got wet before and was not dried by professionals
  • After water damage, a meter shows elevated moisture in the floor, wall or ceiling
  • Swollen trim and baseboards separating at the seams
  • Blistered paint and lifting wallpaper on a wall that got wet
  • Drywall that is soft, swollen or shows moisture staining
  • Rooms that stay humid even with windows open and fans running

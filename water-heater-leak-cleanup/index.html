Water Heater Leak Cleanup Help in Murray

The tank on a water heater stores 40 to 80 gallons, yet a leak can release far more than that. The tank is connected to your supply line, so when the bottom rusts through it refills as fast as it empties. Nothing stops the flow except shutting the cold water valve. How bad it gets depends on placement, whether that is the garage, a closet in the hall or an attic sitting above finished rooms, which is the worst.

In Murray, we start with water extraction and then inspect everything around the heater. Closet walls and the platform under the tank are usually soaked at the bottom. When the heater is in the garage, items kept on the slab get wet and so does the wall adjoining the house. Attic leaks travel downward through the insulation and the ceilings below. Our crew takes readings in every area and sets drying equipment to bring it back to normal.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Turn Off the Heat SourceElectric units get shut down at the breaker. On a gas unit, rotate the gas control to its off setting. Running the burner or the element with no water in the tank is something to avoid every time.
  2. Close the Cold SupplyTurn the valve on the cold pipe above the heater clockwise. If it is stuck or leaking, shut off the main water valve for the house.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterWhat comes out of the tank can cause scalds. Keep children and pets away, and do not wade through pooled water until it has had time to cool.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideA garden hose on the drain valve, if you can get to it safely, lets you send what is left in the tank outside or into a floor drain instead of across the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorLift boxes, rugs and anything absorbent off the wet floor. Cardboard and fabric soak up water quickly and hold it against walls and concrete.
Coverage

What About Insurance?

Water damage to belongings, walls and floors from a tank that fails suddenly is often covered as a sudden and accidental loss. Usually the unit is left out of the claim, as age related wear is viewed as a maintenance matter. Because coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks, a claim may be denied or reduced when the adjuster sees indications the tank leaked for a long time, like old stains or trails of rust. Read your policy, take photos of the damage and the heater, and do not dispose of the old unit before the claim is settled.

Our Process

Our Water Heater Leak Cleanup Process

1

Extract the Water

We remove standing water from the floor, closet or garage and, for attic units, from the overflow pan and ceiling cavity, so the surrounding materials stop absorbing it.

2

Inspect the Surrounding Area

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check the platform, wall bases, adjoining rooms and any ceiling below to find the full extent of the wet area.

Soaked insulation, crumbling drywall and damaged baseboards are taken out. In attics we clear wet insulation so the ceiling joists and drywall below can be dried.

Common Causes

What Causes It

  • Pressure rising too high in the tank because the thermostat failed or no expansion tank was installed.
  • Water discharged when the pressure and temperature relief valve fails or opens.
  • A blocked or rusted drain pan under an attic or closet heater that overflows instead of draining.
  • Corroded inlet and outlet fittings or flex connectors on top of the heater.
  • Loose plumbing connections left after a recent installation or repair.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Attic and Ceilings

When an attic heater leaks, water passes through the insulation and lands on the ceiling underneath. Wet insulation stops insulating and its weight can bring ceiling drywall down.

Heater Closet Drywall

Drywall surrounding a heater closet inside the house wicks water up and lets it through to the neighboring rooms. Baseboards swell and the lower wall stays wet inside.

Items Stored Near the Heater

The first casualties are usually the furniture, boxes and keepsakes stored around the heater in a garage or closet. Mold can develop on damp contents that are not promptly dried or tossed, while cardboard boxes disintegrate.

Shared Garage Wall

As water runs across the garage slab, it wets the drywall and bottom plate of the wall the garage shares with the house and continues into flooring on the other side.

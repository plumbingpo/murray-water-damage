Common Water Damage Issues in Salt Lake City

Water damage does not always arrive as a sudden gush across the kitchen. Below are the situations our crews are called to most often around Salt Lake City, and what each one tends to involve.

Hidden leaks: A pinhole in a supply line or a loose drain fitting can drip inside a wall for months. Clues include flooring that feels spongy, blistered paint and a higher water bill you cannot explain. Thermal imaging and moisture readings let us map how far it spread before anything gets cut open. Left alone, constant dampness rots framing and feeds mold, so a minor drip is still worth fixing now.

Sewage: When a main drain line clogs, wastewater comes back up through floor drains, tubs and toilets on the lowest level. It is typically caused by roots in the line, built-up grease or a municipal sewer overwhelmed during a downpour. This is Category 3 water, which carries bacteria and should not be handled without protective gear. Carpet, padding and other absorbent materials that got wet usually cannot be saved.

Storms: Hard rain can outrun gutters and yard drains, then pool against the house. From there it comes in under doors, through window wells and across garage floors. Floodwater from outside picks up soil, fertilizer and whatever else it crossed, so we treat it as contaminated. Homes across Salt Lake City can see this in any season with a strong enough storm.

Crawlspace: In homes built over a crawl space, few people look underneath, so moisture there builds up unseen. Humidity stays high down there when a pipe drips, the ground cover is damaged or water pools after rain. You may notice cupped floorboards, a stale smell in the living area or insulation hanging loose. Our team removes the water, dries the framing and can point out what is letting moisture in.

Condensate: An air conditioner pulls gallons of water out of the air on a humid day. It is meant to exit through a narrow drain tube, and that tube plugs up with slime and debris. When it backs up, the pan overflows into the ceiling, closet or hallway around the air handler. In Salt Lake City we often find this only after a stain or sagging drywall gives it away, so a yearly drain check helps.

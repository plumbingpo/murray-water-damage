Attic Water Damage in Murray

There are two ways an attic ends up wet. From above, rain enters through failed flashing, worn shingles, or the seals at chimneys and vents. The house itself sends moisture upward too, and in a poorly ventilated attic it condenses on cold sheathing. Whichever it is, few people ever go up and look. In Murray, we inspect the attic, track down the source, and get it dry.

On the typical attic job, soaked insulation is the main issue. Heating and cooling bills go up because cellulose and fiberglass alike give up R-value when wet. Worse, soaked insulation holds water against the ceiling drywall and the framing for weeks. We remove what is saturated, dry the wood with air movers and dehumidifiers, and treat any mold on the rafters or sheathing.

Signs to Look For

  • A bath fan or ceiling light fixture with drips or a stain around it
  • Spots of daylight at a chimney, a vent pipe, or a valley in the roof
  • Dripping, rusted or water marked ducts and metal hardware up in the attic
  • Closets on the top floor, or the attic, that smell musty
  • Brown rings or stains on an upstairs ceiling, often worse after rain
  • Flattened, matted or discolored insulation that stands out from what surrounds it
If Nothing Is Done

The Damage Timeline

First storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

Rain that makes it through the roof falls into the insulation and is absorbed. Because the rooms below show nothing, the leak is missed and no roof repair happens.

24 to 48 hours

Mold May Begin

Mold does very well on wet paper facing and damp lumber when the attic is warm and the air is still. Growth can begin on the sheathing and on the back of the ceiling drywall.

Weeks later

Stains Come Through

Repeated wetting saturates the insulation, and water gets down to the ceiling. Brown rings appear in the room below, and the drywall may start to sag.

After months

Rot Sets In

The sheathing comes apart in layers and no longer grips the roofing nails. Rafters stay damp enough to decay, and mold covers large areas of the roof deck.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

Our inspection covers the flashing points, roof deck, exhaust ducts and vents, and tells us if the water is condensation, a leak, or both. Its full reach is mapped with a thermal camera and meter readings.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Wet Wiring

Cable runs, junction boxes and recessed light housings are all found in attics. Water in or on them is a shock and fire risk, so stay out until the power is checked.

Insulation That Stops Working

Once damp and packed down, insulation does little of its job and might not recover even when dry. Rooms under the wet section run hotter or colder, and energy bills rise.

Mold Under the Roof Deck

Poorly ventilated attics often grow mold across the underside of the sheathing. It can cover a wide area before anyone goes up to look.

Ceiling Collapse

Insulation full of water weighs a lot. Drywall that has been wet from above weakens at the fasteners and can drop suddenly, bringing wet insulation down with it.

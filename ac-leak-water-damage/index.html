AC Leak Water Damage in Murray: What to Know

Cooling a house also dries the air, which means the unit is constantly making water it must get rid of. A pan under the coil catches the drips, and from there a thin pipe empties the pan. When that line clogs with algae and dust, the pan fills and spills over. If the air handler sits in a hallway closet or the attic, that water ends up in the nearby floor, wall or ceiling.

Slow leaks do more damage than people expect. It soaks the stand the unit sits on, climbs into the drywall and spreads beneath the flooring, and by the time you see a mark mold has frequently begun. We find how far the moisture spread, dry the structure and repair what was damaged. Clearing the drain or repairing the unit remains a job for an HVAC technician, and our schedule can fit around theirs.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Turn Off the ACSet the thermostat to off, which stops the unit from producing more water. If water is near the air handler's wiring, turn off its breaker as well.
  2. Avoid Wet Wiring and LightsCeiling lights, outlets and the unit itself are off limits while wet, and stay that way until their circuit has been turned off.
  3. Clear Water From the PanUse a wet-vac or rags to take the standing water out of the pan and stop the spill, provided the pan is safe to reach. Do not crawl across a wet attic to do it.
  4. Soak Up What You CanBlot wet carpet, wipe hard floors, set a bucket under any ceiling drip, and move boxes, clothes and furniture away from the closet or the stained area.
  5. Bring In Two ProsCall us to check how far the water spread, and call an HVAC technician to clear the drain line. Keep the AC off until the repair is made.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • No working float switch to stop the system when the pan filled.
  • A dirty filter or low refrigerant, both of which make the coil ice up.
  • Bare refrigerant lines or ducts that sweat in humid weather and drip on the ceiling.
  • Algae, slime and dust from the coil plugging the condensate drain.
  • Units that rely on a condensate pump, when that pump quits or gets unplugged.
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

Drywall Below the Air Handler

When the air handler is overhead, the ceiling under it takes the water. The ceiling stains first, then sags, and a section soaked for days can fall into the hallway or bedroom below.

Nearby Flooring

Water runs out under the closet door and gets beneath carpet pad, laminate or hardwood. Swollen laminate does not go back, and wet hardwood left alone will cup.

Attic Insulation

Overflow in the attic sinks into the surrounding insulation, which keeps the drywall under it wet. Soaked sections usually come out, since they no longer insulate.

Baseboards and Door Trim

Baseboards and door casings near the closet draw water up from the floor. Because pressed-wood trim puffs up and falls apart when wet, new material goes in.

Insurance

What About Insurance?

Coverage for an AC leak usually comes down to timing. If the pan overflowed suddenly and you acted when you found it, many homeowners policies pay for the resulting water damage. If the adjuster decides the drip went on for weeks or months, it may be treated as a gradual leak or a maintenance issue and denied. Either way, the cost of fixing the AC unit is typically left out. Check your policy, and we can supply readings and photos for the claim.
